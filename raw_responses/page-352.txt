Velleius places Sardanapalus in the thirty-third place from Ninus, differing from Ctesias and Diodorus by only one king, unless there is an error — of which not few are read in that excellent writer, not by his fault, but by that of the copyists. About the duration of the empire there is no less disagreement. That trifler Cephallion [says it lasted] a thousand years; Justin, 1300, which Aurelius Augustinus follows in book XII, ch. Diodorus [gives] more than sixty years more than Justin, but modestly, as George Syncellus says: who, after producing that testimony of Diodorus we cited above, says: «These things Diodorus hands down to us, not even daring so to declare the number of the kings, or the precise quantity of the time of their affairs, and that there was a course of times before Troy. He only declared that the time of the 35 kings from Ninus is more than 1[000] years», etc. Aemilius Sura, an old writer [cited] in Velleius Paterculus, reckons from the beginning of Ninus to the destruction of Carthage by Scipio Africanus the Younger, 1995 years.
